# Art direction and provenance

Highway Phantom is a sunset car-window action daydream. The player camera belongs to the child's moving car; the runner stays alongside it. The reference trailer supplied by the user informed the opening and premise. It is preserved unmodified.

The background, opening illustration, and character artwork were generated with the built-in image-generation tool. The current runner uses crisp cel-shaded armor components in assets/runner_rig.png, replacing the earlier painted and pixel animation sheets. Its prompt sequence is recorded in assets/runner_rig_prompts.md. The renderer assembles one consistent character with continuous joint motion and fixed limb lengths. A runtime chroma-key shader removes the atlas backing; discarded source variants remain in the generation history. No third-party game sprites or sound samples are used. The synthesized score and effects are generated by tools/make_audio.py. The world, car frame, enemies, platforms, particles and UI are drawn in Godot.

## Final assets and prompts

### assets/runner_rig.png — current connected character

Built-in image generation produced twelve separate armor components for one original cobalt, ivory and navy cyber runner. Source rectangles and joint anchors live in scripts/runner_parts.gd. The same head, torso, pelvis, legs, arms, cannon, saber and scarf are used across every movement. The running gait alternates support and swing; planted feet move at the road's exact speed, and curved recovery paths lift the heel and bring the knee forward. Two-bone geometry preserves limb proportions through the entire cycle and sword swing. Jump, landing and dash use continuous pose weights. Weapons animate over the gait, and the scarf deforms continuously. Previous animation sheets below are retained as development history and are no longer used by the game.

The revised gait uses shorter, broader armored legs, restrained ankle rotation, a low heel recovery, and less vertical body bounce. The sword now has three authored clips in scripts/sword_animation.gd: downstroke, rising return, and overhead finisher. Hand position, blade angle, torso lean, pelvis shift and cannon bracing follow each attack. Chain points share a pose so one cut flows into the next. Buffered presses survive hit pauses, each attack damages a target only once, and the third hit has stronger damage, impact and sound. The visible saber sweep drives contact checks and its fading ribbon. Captures/three-slash-poses.png can be regenerated with tools/preview_combo.gd for visual review.

### assets/runner_dash_pixel.png — dash poses

Generated with the built-in image-generation tool using the current pixel runner as the character reference. The full prompt and source are in assets/runner_dash_prompts.md. Eight ground poses cover entry, push-off, acceleration, boost, release and recovery; four air poses keep the torso registered while the legs tuck. The 0.23-second boost eases into and out of speed, followed by an interruptible 0.12-second visual recovery. Ground recovery joins the matching running stride, while air recovery follows the jump's vertical motion. Cannon recoil remains articulated. Afterimages retain the pose they were emitted with, drift with the roadside, and fade within 0.16 seconds. The runner stays visible throughout the dash instead of blinking for invulnerability.

### assets/runner_jump_pixel.png — jump poses

Generated with the built-in image-generation tool using the current pixel runner as a style and character reference. The full prompt is in assets/runner_jump_prompts.md. Six airborne poses cover push-off, rising, tuck, apex, descent, and reaching for contact. Their torso registration stays stable as the feet tuck. Animation follows vertical velocity with a minimum pose duration; a double jump folds back through the poses. Landing uses a short, continuous compression and returns the existing running cycle to its contact frame. Physics, jump response, and the slower distance-driven running cadence are preserved.
